## Why do the French eat foie gras at Christmas?

**In France, foie gras is the dish of the Christmas and New Year table, and most of the year's foie gras is sold in that festive season.** Estimates in the French press put the festive season at around 70 to 80 percent of annual sales: [Que Choisir](https://www.quechoisir.org/enquete-foies-gras-l-enquete-n23197/) wrote in 2016 that 80 percent of sales happen around Christmas, and [Gault&Millau](https://fr.gaultmillau.com/fr/news/d-ou-nous-vient-cette-habitude-de-consommer-du-foie-gras-a-noel), citing Statista in 2024, says producers make 70 to 75 percent of their annual sales over Christmas and New Year. In a CSA survey for CIFOG, the French foie gras interprofession, 73 percent of French people called foie gras a must at festive meals, as [Réussir reported](https://www.reussir.fr/articles/foie-gras-les-prix-restent-preserves-pour-les-fetes-2026) on 5 November 2026.

Served on toast as a starter, foie gras is how a French réveillon, the Christmas Eve or New Year's Eve dinner, often begins. At Bisou Phuket, the foie gras terrine and the French toast with foie gras play that part on the current menu (as of 23 November 2026), not only in December.

## What exactly is foie gras?

**French law defines foie gras as the liver of a duck or goose specially fattened by gavage, and has protected it as part of the national heritage since 2006.** Article L654-27-1 of the French rural code, created by the agricultural law of 5 January 2006, states that foie gras belongs to the cultural and culinary heritage protected in France, and defines it as "le foie d'un canard ou d'une oie spécialement engraissé par gavage", the liver of a duck or goose specially fattened by gavage, the traditional method. The text is on [Légifrance](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000006584967), and in 2022 the Ministry of Agriculture cited it in an [answer to a Senate written question](https://www.senat.fr/questions/base/2022/qSEQ220126098.html).

It is served two ways: cold, as a terrine, the form the Bisou terrine with French toast takes, or warm, seared quickly in a pan.

## What wine goes with foie gras?

**Foie gras with a sweet white wine such as Sauternes is the classic French pairing.** The French merchant [iDealwine](https://www.idealwine.net/que-boire-avec-le-foie-gras-accords-mets-vins-foie-gras) calls Sauternes with foie gras "the great classic", and wine writer [Britt Karlsson](https://www.bkwine.com/news/sauternes-wine-and-foie-gras-a-winning-combination/) describes foie gras with a sweet wine as a classic French combination. iDealwine also suggests Champagne, or a sweet Alsace or Jurançon that keeps some acidity, to keep the palate fresh across several plates.

The Bisou Phuket list runs from Jura, the Loire, the Rhône and Alsace to Bordeaux and Burgundy, with whites from THB 1,700 a bottle as of 23 November 2026. The team will point you to a bottle for the plates you choose, and the cocktail bar is open until midnight.

## What if someone at the table does not eat foie gras?

**The rest of the Bisou Phuket menu needs no foie gras at all, and vegetarian and vegan guests are well looked after.** There are plenty of vegetarian dishes, and the kitchen prepares dedicated plates for vegan guests. For the same French generosity without foie gras, the table can share the black truffle pasta (THB 790), the whole seabass in salt crust flambéed at the table (THB 1,690) or the Prunier caviar 30 g with French toast (THB 2,990), prices as of 23 November 2026.
