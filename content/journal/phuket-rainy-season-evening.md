**In Phuket the September rain is a daytime event, and the evening is the driest part of the day.** Across eleven Septembers of half-hourly observations at Phuket International Airport, rain was falling in 19.8 percent of afternoon readings and 19.7 percent of morning ones, against 15.1 percent in the evening: the wettest hour of a September day is 2 PM and the driest is 10 PM. So the useful answer to what to do in Phuket when it rains is to give the afternoon to something indoors and give the evening back to dinner, booked, in a room with a roof.

We run a dining room in Cherngtalay and we stay open right through the rainy season, so this is written from the west coast rather than from a weather app. Everything below is dated, every outside number names where it came from, and the numbers we could not find published anywhere we counted ourselves.

## The short answer, from eleven years of airport records

**The evening is the driest part of a September day in Phuket, and the middle of the afternoon is the wettest.** Rain was falling in 21.9 percent of readings taken at 2 PM and in 13.8 percent of readings taken at 10 PM.

| September, by part of day | Share of readings with rain falling |
| --- | --- |
| Morning, 6 AM to noon | 19.7 percent |
| Afternoon, noon to 6 PM | 19.8 percent |
| Evening, 6 PM to midnight | 15.1 percent |
| Night, midnight to 6 AM | 16.7 percent |

Across the hours a dinner actually occupies, 6 PM to 1 AM, rain was falling in 15.3 percent of September readings. Put the other way round: on a September evening on this island, it is not raining about five times out of six.

**How we counted.** We downloaded every routine observation recorded at Phuket International Airport, which sits in Thalang, the same district as Cherngtalay and Bang Tao, from the [Iowa Environmental Mesonet](https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/request/download.phtml) archive: 189,422 half-hourly readings between January 2015 and December 2025, timestamped in Thai time, of which 15,572 fall in the eleven Septembers behind every September figure above. A reading counts as wet when the observer logged rain in the present-weather codes. That measures how often rain was falling at the airport, not how much fell, and the airport is twenty minutes up the coast rather than outside your window. It is still the closest thing to an hourly record of Phuket rain that exists in public: neither the Thai Meteorological Department nor the World Meteorological Organization publishes a time-of-day breakdown for the island.

## Does it rain all day in Phuket?

**No. In eleven Septembers the airport recorded 808 separate spells of rain, and the median one lasted an hour.** Sixty one percent were over within the hour, 78 percent within two hours, 87 percent within three.

Rain fell at some point on 78 percent of September days, which sounds relentless until you look at the shape of those days: on a wet one, rain was observed in about six of the twenty four hours. A rainy day in Phuket is a dry day with holes in it.

Willy Thuan, who has lived on Phuket since 1994 and has published [Phuket 101](https://www.phuket101.net/phuket-weather/) since 2011, puts the showers in the back half of the day: they "come in the afternoon or evening", he writes. The airport record splits those two halves, and the split is the useful part, because the afternoon carries most of the rain and the evening carries noticeably less of it. His [rainy-season page](https://www.phuket101.net/rainy-season/) is also honest about the exception, and so are we: it records stretches of three, six, even nine days when the rain barely lets up. Nobody can forecast which week that will be, and if it lands on yours, no article can fix it.

## September and October in numbers

**September is Phuket's wettest month on the long-run average, at 350 to 400 millimetres across 22 rain days, and October is close behind.** Those are the Thai Meteorological Department's own normals for the province, measured over 1991 to 2020.

The department keeps two stations on the island and they do not agree with each other, which matters if you are staying on this coast. Phuket Town averages 2,277.3 millimetres a year over 176 rain days. Thalang, the district that contains Cherngtalay, Bang Tao and Laguna, averages 2,662.8 millimetres over 186 rain days. Thalang is the wetter of the two, by about 385 millimetres a year.

Three more numbers worth carrying:

- **The light goes early.** Sunset in Phuket falls from 6:34 PM on 1 September to 6:05 PM on 31 October, and civil twilight ends about twenty minutes after that, so usable light is gone before 7 PM every night of both months. Sunrise barely moves. The season takes its light off the evening end.
- **September is the dimmest month of the year**, at about three and a half hours of sunshine a day against seven and a half in January, on the 1991 to 2020 averages published by [Climates to Travel](https://www.climatestotravel.com/climate/thailand/phuket).
- **This particular season is forecast in two directions.** The Thai Meteorological Department's three-month outlook, issued 24 August 2026, puts September on Thailand's southern west coast about 20 percent above that region's own normal, at 490 to 540 millimetres, and October about 30 percent below it, at 230 to 280 millimetres. That outlook covers the whole coast from Ranong to Satun, whose baseline runs higher than Phuket's own station normal, so read it as a direction rather than as a Phuket figure. Across the September to November quarter it lands near the long-run average.

One reassurance belongs here, because it is the fear underneath the search. Phuket is not a storm coast. In the Thai Meteorological Department's tropical cyclone record for the 72 years from 1951 to 2022, exactly two cyclones reached the province, both of them weak depressions and both in November.

## October is not September

**In October the rain moves into the evening, and the observers log it as heavy more often.** In the same airport record, October readings carrying a heavy-rain or thunderstorm code peak at 6 PM and 7 PM, at roughly twice the September evening rate.

The practical difference is one hour of your planning. A September shower usually lands while you are still deciding what to do with the afternoon. An October one can land exactly as you are leaving for dinner. In October, book the earlier seating, take a car rather than a scooter, and treat 6 PM to 8 PM as the part of the evening to spend indoors rather than in transit.

October also carries this coast's more disruptive recent weather. The worst single day in recent years came in October, not September: torrential rain on 22 October 2025 triggered flash floods and a landslide that closed Patong Hill, about 20 kilometres down the same coast, and the same night's rain flooded roads in Cherngtalay, Kathu and Mueang. It is a fair warning against treating October as the safe month.

## What the rainy day guides answer, and what they leave out

**Every well-ranked rainy-day guide for Phuket answers for the afternoon, and stops.** The most authoritative of them, Phuket 101's [rainy-day list](https://www.phuket101.net/things-to-do-in-phuket-when-it-rains/), was refreshed on 2 September 2026 and lists 23 things to do. Not one of them is a restaurant.

We went through that list and sorted it by the clock, because that is the fact a visitor needs and none of these pages provides:

- **Five** of the 23 publish hours that run past 8 PM: two shopping centres, a golf studio, an ice rink, and a cat cafe that shuts at 8:30 PM.
- **Six** publish hours that end at 7 PM or earlier, the aquarium and the water park among them.
- **Twelve** publish no hours at all.

The latest closing time anywhere on that list is 10 PM. Put it next to the same publisher's own account of when the rain comes, a heavy shower by 3 or 4 PM lasting 30 minutes to two hours, and the arithmetic is plain: the shower passes, the light goes at 6:20, the list runs out at 10, and nobody has told you where to have dinner.

The gap is wider still for this coast. That page's entire answer for anyone staying near Bang Tao or Laguna is a single sentence pointing at Porto de Phuket or Boat Avenue. Both are open-air complexes: the shops are enclosed, the walkways between them are not.

Tripadvisor's rainy-day page, which ranks near the top for the same question, is not an article at all. It is an attractions filter, and Tripadvisor files restaurants in a separate section, so by construction that page cannot contain a dinner. None of this is bad work. Phuket 101 in particular is careful, current, and written by someone who has lived here for three decades. It simply answers a different question from the one asked at six in the evening with wet hair.

## What the rain actually takes away: the sea and the boats

**The monsoon does not take the island away from you, it takes the water away.** From May to October the Andaman swell reaches this coast, red flags go up, and swimming at Bang Tao, Surin and Layan becomes genuinely unsafe rather than merely unpleasant.

Treat a red flag as a closure, not a warning. Rip currents on this coast are strong enough to catch confident swimmers, and provincial authorities are consolidating the island’s lifeguard services in response to drownings at its beaches. The surf that causes them is the same surf that looks manageable from a sunbed.

The boats are affected too, and the closures are published rather than improvised:

- **Mu Ko Similan and Mu Ko Surin national parks are closed from 15 May to 15 October 2026.** A September trip to the Similans is not weather-dependent, it is not happening.
- **Maya Bay is closed from 1 August to 30 September 2026** for its annual rehabilitation, reopening on 1 October, though Phi Phi boat tours themselves continue to run.
- **Small boats get ordered ashore by the Phuket Marine Office**, not by your operator. Through late August and into September 2026 the Thai Meteorological Department repeatedly warned of two to three metre waves in the upper Andaman and told small craft to stay in port.

That is the honest shape of a wet-season day here. The daytime menu is shorter and less reliable than the brochure suggests. The evening menu is untouched.

## How to plan an evening around a shower

**The single best move is to stop leaving the evening to chance: book the table, go earlier than you would in February, and arrive by car.** Everything else follows from that.

- **Book rather than walk in.** In low season it is tempting to assume everywhere is empty. The rooms that are genuinely good on this coast are small, and a shower sends everyone into them at the same moment.
- **Take a car, and know what it will cost.** Both major apps publish, in their own words, that fares rise when demand outruns available drivers, which is exactly what a downpour produces. Grab explains it on its own [pricing page](https://www.grab.com/inside-grab/stories/surge-dynamic-pricing-explained/), and Bolt lists extreme weather among the conditions that raise its rates.
- **Do not count on the local buses after dinner.** The blue songthaew on the Kamala, Surin and Bang Tao route finishes around 6 PM, the hour dinner starts. The Phuket Smart Bus is a flat 100 baht and stops at Cherng Talay, Laguna and Surin, but its last departure from the airport is 11:30 PM heading south, only a few runs a day call at Laguna, and no late service is published coming back. It is a bus in the rain, not a plan.
- **Allow more time on the road.** Phuket 101 names the Cherngtalay road between Ban Kian junction and Blue Tree among the island's five most flood-prone stretches, and on 20 May 2026 The Phuket News reported water deep enough to stop motorcycles on Soi Cherng Talay 4. It drains quickly once the rain stops. It still costs you twenty minutes.
- **Ask where to park.** On a wet night, the difference between a good arrival and a bad one is fifty metres of open ground.

## What to wear when the room is cool and the air is not

**Dress for two climates, because a wet Phuket evening has both.** Outside it is around 30 degrees at 82 percent humidity in September. Inside, every dining room on the island runs its air conditioning hard, and a damp shirt in a cold room is the reason people leave early.

Bring something light over the shoulders. Choose shoes that survive a puddle, because the fifty metres between the car and the door is the wettest part of the evening. And our own dress code is smart casual with no flip-flops, which is common on this coast and worth knowing if the day started on the sand.

## Is September or October still worth it?

**Yes, if you come for the island rather than for the beach, and the numbers on quietness are unarguable.** Phuket International Airport handled 1,016,543 passengers in September 2025, its quietest month of the year, against 2,000,332 in January 2025. That is almost exactly half.

Hotels tell the same story from the other end. Knight Frank put island-wide occupancy at 91.8 percent in January 2025 and 66.9 percent in June, the low point of that half-year. Phuket 101 estimates low-season room rates run 40 to 60 percent below peak, and while that is one publisher's estimate rather than survey data, the direction is not in dispute. Thailand's own tourism authority markets the season deliberately now, under a green-season campaign launched in July 2026, on the grounds that the country is greener and calmer this way.

So the trade is legible. You give up the swimming, the Similans and a certain amount of blue sky. You get an island handling half the passengers it handles in January, hotel rooms well below the January rate, restaurants where a table is available, and the best sunsets of the year on the evenings when the clouds break.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is there to do in Phuket at night when it rains?

Dinner is the answer the rainy-day guides leave out. On Phuket 101's rainy-day list for the island, the best-ranked guide for the question, every one of the 23 entries that publishes its hours closes by 10 PM at the latest, six close by 7 PM, and twelve publish no hours at all. Restaurants, cocktail bars and hotel bars are what remain after that, and on the west coast the indoor rooms set back from the beach are unaffected by weather that empties the beach clubs.

### Does it rain all day in Phuket?

Rarely. In eleven Septembers of observations at Phuket International Airport, the median spell of rain lasted one hour, 78 percent were over within two hours, and although rain fell at some point on 78 percent of September days, it was observed in only about six hours of a wet day. The exception is real: every season brings stretches of three to nine days of continuous rain.

### What time of day does it rain in Phuket?

Mostly the afternoon. In the airport record for September, the wettest hour of the day is 2 PM, at 21.9 percent of readings, and the driest is 10 PM, at 13.8 percent. October behaves differently: heavy rain and thunderstorms there peak at 6 PM and 7 PM, so an October evening is more likely to start wet than a September one.

### Is Phuket worth visiting in September?

Yes, if the beach is not the whole point of the trip. September is the wettest month on the Thai Meteorological Department's 1991 to 2020 normals, at 350 to 400 millimetres over 22 rain days, and the west coast sea is under red flags. It is also the quietest month of the year, with Phuket airport handling 1,016,543 passengers in September 2025 against 2,000,332 in January 2025, and hotel rates well below peak.

### Is October a bad time to go to Phuket?

No. The Thai Meteorological Department's outlook issued 24 August 2026 forecast October on Thailand's southern west coast at about 30 percent below normal rainfall. The catch is timing rather than volume: in the airport record, October's heavy rain concentrates in the early evening, peaking at 6 PM and 7 PM, so plan the earlier seating and take a car.

### Where can you eat in Bang Tao when it rains?

Indoors, and away from the sand. Beach clubs on Bang Tao and Layan are open air, and Boat Avenue and Porto de Phuket are open-air complexes where the shops are enclosed but the walkways are not. The dining rooms along Bandon-Cherngtalay Road are fully indoors, and most kitchens on this coast take their last order between 10 and 11 PM. Bisou Phuket, on Bandon-Cherngtalay Road, serves until around 11 PM with the bar open to 1 AM.

### Should you book a restaurant in advance in Phuket in low season?

Yes, and especially when it is raining. Low occupancy across the island does not mean an empty dining room: a shower sends everyone indoors at the same moment, and the good rooms on this coast are small. Booking also lets you ask for the table you want and tell the restaurant you are arriving by car.

### Can you swim at Bang Tao Beach in September?

Not when the red flags are up, which is most of the southwest monsoon between May and October. The Andaman swell reaches this coast in those months and the rip currents are strong enough to catch confident swimmers, which is why provincial authorities are consolidating the island’s lifeguard services. Hotel pools stay open, and the sea is worth waiting for until November.
